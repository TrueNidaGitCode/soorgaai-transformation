# AI Center of Excellence

**Domain:** AI Strategy  
**Category:** Organizational Capability

---

## Purpose

Defines the structure, operating model, and responsibilities of an AI Center of Excellence (AI CoE)
for automotive enterprises undergoing AI transformation.

---

## What is an AI CoE

An AI Center of Excellence is a centralized organizational function that:

- Defines enterprise AI standards, practices, and tools
- Builds and maintains reusable AI capabilities and platforms
- Supports business domains with AI expertise and guidance
- Governs AI initiative quality and ethical compliance
- Drives continuous improvement of the AI transformation program

---

## CoE Operating Models

### Centralized CoE
All AI engineering talent is pooled in a central team.  
Best for: Early-stage transformation, small-to-medium enterprises.  
Risk: Bottleneck as demand grows; perceived as detached from business domains.

### Hub-and-Spoke CoE
A central CoE (hub) provides platforms, standards, and expertise.
Domain AI teams (spokes) execute domain-specific initiatives.  
Best for: Large automotive OEMs with multiple engineering domains.  
This is the recommended model for automotive enterprises.

### Federated CoE
Lightweight central governance with strong domain autonomy.  
Best for: Mature AI organizations with established domain capabilities.

---

## Hub-and-Spoke Structure (Recommended)

### Central Hub Responsibilities
- AI platform and infrastructure
- Enterprise AI standards and governance
- Common tooling (MLOps, data platforms, model registry)
- AI talent development and training programs
- Cross-domain knowledge management
- External partnerships (academic, vendor, regulatory)

### Domain Spoke Responsibilities
- Domain-specific AI use case delivery
- Adapting enterprise standards to domain constraints
- Feedback loop to central hub on emerging needs
- Functional safety compliance in safety-critical domains

---

## CoE Maturity Levels

| Level | Description | Indicators |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| 1 — Emerging | Ad hoc AI projects, no central coordination | Individual team experiments |
| 2 — Establishing | CoE formed, standards being defined | First shared platform deployed |
| 3 — Operating | CoE fully operational, hub-and-spoke active | >5 domain initiatives running |
| 4 — Scaling | Enterprise-wide AI adoption, continuous improvement | CoE self-sustaining |
| 5 — Leading | AI CoE drives competitive differentiation | AI embedded in all business processes |

---

## Automotive CoE Considerations

- The CoE must bridge traditional automotive engineering and software/AI practices
- Functional safety awareness is required for CoE members supporting ADAS and Validation domains
- ASPICE compliance and integration with existing quality management systems is essential
- The CoE should engage early with regulatory affairs on EU AI Act compliance
