# Purpose

Within the artificial intelligence industry and AI strategy domain, the AI ROI capability focuses on rigorously quantifying the value derived from AI initiatives relative to investment—both financial and strategic—using industry-aware metrics. It expands the core goal of measuring business value by accounting for AI-specific factors like benefit timing uncertainty, outcome decay, and transformation-led returns.

---

# Artificial Intelligence Business Context

Companies in the AI industry fall into two relevant sub‑categories: (1) AI platform and tool providers—developers of AI models, frameworks and deployment platforms, whose ROI focus is on monetization, product-market fit, customer adoption and usage-based revenue; (2) AI‑strategic adopters—enterprises across sectors embedding AI into operations, workflows, or product offerings, where ROI measurement must capture revenue uplift, cost savings, operational transformation and longer-term strategic shifts.

---

# Typical Artificial Intelligence Business Challenges

- Benefit timing uncertainty
- Value decay over time
- Undefined ROI for soft outcomes
- Data silos and poor baselines
- Inconsistent measurement frameworks
- Pilot-to-scale value erosion

---

# Typical Artificial Intelligence Workflows

- Define value‑up‑front → Set multi‑layer metrics → Measure over time → Compute ROI
- Baseline current metrics → Launch AI pilot → Track operational and financial outcomes → Update ROI narrative
- Segment ROI by mechanism (productivity, engagement, strategic) → Aggregate into portfolio view → Inform funding decisions

---

# Typical AI Opportunities

- Applying eROI framework: value-if-successful, likelihood, investment required
- Using multi‑axis value matrix: financial outcomes × value mechanisms
- Adopting portfolio-level ROI index combining return, revenue growth, cost savings, speed
- Shifting from output metrics to business outcome metrics
- Embedding continuous ROI tracking to catch value decay

---

# Artificial Intelligence Principles

- Use industry‑grounded frameworks (e.g. eROI, value‑matrix) that separate value timing and mechanism
- Prioritize measurable outcomes over outputs—define revenue, efficiency, strategic metrics upfront
- Measure continuously, not at a single point—guard against decay and capture full lifecycle value
- Align measurement approaches across pilots and scaled deployments using a shared language
- Treat AI initiatives as portfolios—balance short‑term productivity with long‑term strategic bets

---

# Leadership Question

AI Platform Provider: "How effectively are we converting AI usage into monetizable value, and how can we measure that continuously?"; AI‑Strategic Adopter: "Are we capturing both short‑term efficiency gains and long‑term strategic value from our AI investments in a reliable, measurable way?"
