# Purpose

Within the AI industry, AI Implementation Prioritization enables organizations to objectively score and rank AI use cases by assessing business value, technical feasibility, data readiness, and organizational impact, ensuring that investment decisions align with realistic execution capability. This capability operationalizes the core objective of prioritizing use cases to drive ROI while managing risk and delivery confidence.

---

# Artificial Intelligence Business Context

The AI industry includes:
• AI-first technology vendors and consulting firms that propose AI solutions to enterprise clients, where prioritization helps design client roadmaps and build trust quickly.
• Enterprises building internal AI capabilities, spanning functions like data science, IT, product, and compliance, where prioritization ensures internal use cases deliver measurable outcomes and scalable value.

---

# Typical Artificial Intelligence Business Challenges

- Excitement-driven use case selection
- Insufficient data readiness
- Overly ambitious scope
- Lack of cross‑function alignment
- Unclear ROI or metric
- Governance or compliance risk

---

# Typical Artificial Intelligence Workflows

- Brainstorm use cases → Score on value, feasibility, data readiness → Plot on value‑feasibility matrix → Select quick wins and strategic bets
- Gather cross‑functional scoring committee → Apply weighted scoring (business impact, feasibility, data readiness, org readiness) → Rank and sequence use cases
- Define scope → Estimate Value‑if‑Successful, Likelihood‑of‑Success, Investment Required (eROI) → Compute expected ROI → Construct portfolio
- Use BXT framework: evaluate Business viability, User Experience, Technical feasibility → Combine with strategic fit → Plot on prioritization chart

---

# Typical AI Opportunities

- Quick‑win identification: high‑value, high‑feasibility projects to build momentum
- Strategic bets: allocate ambitious, high‑value, lower feasibility use cases into roadmap stages
- Future portfolio planning: park ideas lacking data readiness until infrastructure improves
- eROI‑based portfolio design: evaluating value‑if‑successful, likelihood, and investment separately
- Integrated frameworks: BXT or weighted scoring with business, feasibility, data, org readiness aligners

---

# Artificial Intelligence Principles

- Always include cross‑functional stakeholders (business, data, technology, risk) in scoring to avoid blind spots
- Treat data readiness as a gating criterion, not just another weight
- Start with high business impact and feasibility, not novelty or excitement
- Use simple weighted scoring but emphasize conversation as much as the numeric result
- Sequence use cases into quick wins, strategic bets, and deferred park‑backs based on scoring outputs

# Leadership Question

AI‑first vendor / consulting context: "Which client use cases can deliver measurable business value quickly, with existing data and technical capabilities?"
Enterprise internal AI context: "Which internal AI initiatives should we prioritize now to deliver ROI, build trust, and enable scalable capability across functions?"
