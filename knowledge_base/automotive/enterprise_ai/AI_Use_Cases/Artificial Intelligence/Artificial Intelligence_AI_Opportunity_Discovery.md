# Purpose

Within the Artificial Intelligence industry, AI Opportunity Discovery involves systematically identifying and documenting where AI can address tangible business pain points, contextualized for AI use cases. It extends the core capability by focusing on mapping operational friction, validating feasibility through real organizational data, and distinguishing truly viable AI applications from hype.

---

# Artificial Intelligence Business Context

In the AI industry focused on AI Use Cases, there are two relevant company types: • AI-first product vendors: companies building platforms or tools that embed AI (e.g., recommendation engines, NLP services), seeking discovery to fuel their next offerings. • AI adopters: enterprises across sectors (finance, healthcare, manufacturing) identifying internal use cases to improve operations, customer service, or innovation.

---

# Typical Artificial Intelligence Business Challenges

- Data quality and readiness
- Fragmented legacy systems
- Lack of clear AI ROI
- Unvalidated hype-driven ideas
- Discovery dependent on manual workshops
- Stakeholder misunderstanding of AI potential

---

# Typical Artificial Intelligence Workflows

- Pain point identification → Feasibility assessment → Opportunity documentation
- Operational friction mapping → Data readiness check → Prioritization
- Executive-led use case workshop → Quick prototype validation → ROI estimation

---

# Common High-Effort Activities

- Manual stakeholder interviews and mapping
- Workshops with broad aspirations rather than validated needs
- Data exploration and cleanup to test hypotheses
- Drafting business cases on unvalidated ideas

---

# Typical AI Opportunities

- For AI‑first product vendors: spotting unmet customer needs via voice-of-customer and designing AI features that solve them
- For AI adopters: surfacing operational friction points where AI can automate or augment tasks, then validating with quick prototypes
- Filtering out hype-driven ideas through desirability, feasibility, viability lenses

---

# Artificial Intelligence Principles

- Prioritize opportunities grounded in real operational friction—not vendor demos
- Use rapid validation (e.g. quick prototype or small-scale test) before documenting opportunity
- Frame opportunities through the lenses of desirability, feasibility, viability
- Differentiate between AI‑first vendors and adopters to align discovery to product market vs. internal impact

---

# Leadership Question

AI‑first vendor: "What unaddressed customer tasks are causing friction today that AI could solve effectively?" AI adopter: "Where in our operations are staff spending time on repetitive, error‑prone tasks that AI could augment with validated ROI?"
