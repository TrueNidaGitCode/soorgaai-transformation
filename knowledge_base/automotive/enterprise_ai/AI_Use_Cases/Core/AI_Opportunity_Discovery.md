# AI Opportunity Discovery

## Purpose

AI Opportunity Discovery is the first stage of the AI use case pipeline. It converts a business problem statement — expressed in the user's own words — into a structured AI opportunity with clearly documented workflow context, pain points, and repetitive activities that AI can address.

For project and product managers, this capability answers the foundational question: is this business problem a genuine AI opportunity, and can we articulate it precisely enough to take forward?

---

# Core Principles

Every AI use case begins with a real business problem.

1. Business Problem Identification
2. Workflow & Activity Analysis
3. Opportunity Signal Recognition

These three principles guide the discovery process from a vague pain point to a well-formed AI opportunity statement.

---

# 1. Business Problem Identification

## Definition

Business Problem Identification is the process of understanding, in precise terms, what problem the delivery team is experiencing. It distinguishes between the symptom — what people notice and complain about — and the root cause, which is the actual target for AI intervention.

A well-identified business problem has a clear owner, a measurable impact, and sufficient frequency to justify AI investment. Problems that are rare, politically sensitive, or caused by process failure rather than cognitive effort are typically not strong AI candidates.

The discovery process starts with listening to delivery teams describe their day-to-day pain. AI opportunities are almost always found in work that is repetitive, knowledge-intensive, time-consuming, and currently done manually.

## Key Principles

* Listen to how delivery teams describe their pain — use their language, not AI terminology.
* Identify who is doing the work, how often, and what they are doing manually.
* Separate symptoms from root causes before proposing any AI solution.
* Validate that the problem recurs at a frequency worth automating.
* Confirm that the problem is caused by cognitive effort, not by missing process or tools.
* Document the problem in one sentence: who does what, how often, and with what effort.

## Leadership Question

**What specific task or decision in our current workflow consumes the most time, requires the most expertise, and is most frequently repeated?**

# 2. Workflow & Activity Analysis

## Definition

Workflow and Activity Analysis maps the current manual process in enough detail to identify precisely where AI assistance can be applied. It is not a full process engineering exercise — it is a targeted analysis of the high-effort activities within a workflow that are candidates for AI augmentation.

The output is a clear description of the current state: the sequence of steps, the inputs each step requires, the decisions made at each step, the skills needed, and the time typically consumed. This baseline is essential for measuring the value of AI intervention later in the pipeline.

Activities with high knowledge requirements, multiple data sources, and significant variation between cases are consistently the strongest AI opportunities. Activities that are simple, rules-based, or already well-automated are typically not.

## Key Principles

* Map the current workflow at the activity level — not the process or system level.
* Identify which activities require expert judgment and tacit knowledge.
* Note the data sources, tools, and inputs each activity depends on.
* Estimate the time consumed per activity and the frequency of the workflow.
* Identify handover points where work is passed between people or teams.
* Highlight activities where quality varies significantly between individuals.
* Separate human judgment activities from rule-based or automatable steps.

## Leadership Question

**What are the specific steps in this workflow that depend on expert knowledge, consume significant time, or produce inconsistent outcomes between team members?**

# 3. Opportunity Signal Recognition

## Definition

Opportunity Signal Recognition is the judgment step that determines whether a workflow activity is a genuine AI opportunity. Not all painful activities are suitable for AI — some require process redesign, better tooling, or organizational change rather than AI intervention.

AI opportunities share a consistent set of signals: they are repetitive, involve pattern recognition across large volumes of data, require knowledge that can be learned from historical examples, and currently depend on expert time that is scarce or expensive. When these signals are present together, AI assistance is likely to be both feasible and valuable.

The output of this step is a clearly stated AI opportunity that names the activity, describes the AI intervention type, and explains why AI is the right approach rather than an alternative solution.

## Key Principles

* Validate that the activity involves pattern recognition, classification, or prediction — not just data retrieval.
* Confirm that historical examples of the activity exist in structured or semi-structured form.
* Check whether the activity requires knowledge that could be learned from past cases.
* Identify whether the bottleneck is human cognitive effort or system/process constraint.
* Articulate the AI intervention type: classification, generation, summarisation, prediction, or recommendation.
* State the opportunity clearly: AI can assist with X by doing Y, reducing Z effort.
* Flag activities that are better solved by process improvement or better tooling.

## Leadership Question

**Is this activity a genuine AI opportunity — does it involve pattern recognition from historical data, and is the bottleneck human cognitive effort rather than a missing process or tool?**
