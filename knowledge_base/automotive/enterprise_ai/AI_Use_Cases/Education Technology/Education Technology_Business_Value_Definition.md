# Purpose

This layer supplies education technology context for quantifying the value of
an AI initiative: which measures matter in this industry, what the realistic
magnitudes are, and which apparent benefits do not survive contact with how
these businesses actually make money.

Valuation method and output structure are inherited from the Core Asset.

---

## Business Value Definition

Education businesses are small-margin, high-touch, and seasonal. Value has to
be expressed in terms an owner recognises: hours returned to teaching, students
retained, fees collected on time. Abstract productivity gains do not translate.

### The four value levers that matter

**1. Retention.** The dominant lever. A student who leaves takes their entire
remaining lifetime value with them, and replacing them costs acquisition effort
in a market that is local and word-of-mouth driven. In subscription EdTech, the
equivalent is institutional churn. Anything that identifies disengagement early
is measured here.

**2. Staff time returned to teaching.** Administrative hours are not merely
costs; in owner-operated institutions they are hours not spent on the revenue-
generating activity. Value is the hours saved multiplied by what those hours
otherwise produce, which is higher than a clerical wage.

**3. Collection rate and timing.** Fees owed but uncollected are the most
common working-capital problem in this industry. Improving collection speed
converts existing revenue into available cash without adding a single student.

**4. Capacity utilisation.** Empty slots in a timetable are unrecoverable
revenue. Better scheduling and forecasting fill them.

---

# Measures That Work in This Industry

- Students retained per term, and time-to-detection of disengagement
- Administrative hours per instructor per week
- Days sales outstanding on fees; proportion collected within the term
- Class fill rate against capacity
- Enquiry-to-enrolment conversion, and time to first response
- Instructor time spent on records versus instruction
- Parent-communication coverage: proportion of families reached per cycle

---

# Measures That Mislead in This Industry

- **Messages generated.** Volume is not value; an unread message costs
  attention and can damage the relationship.
- **Model accuracy in isolation.** A 95%-accurate attrition flag that nobody
  acts on is worth nothing. Value accrues at the intervention, not the
  prediction.
- **Dashboard engagement.** Owner-operators do not use dashboards regularly, so
  low usage says nothing about whether the underlying insight was valuable.
- **Aggregate time saved.** Minutes saved across scattered tasks do not
  reassemble into a usable hour. Value is credible only where the saving is
  concentrated in a single recurring task.

---

# Realistic Magnitudes

Anchor estimates conservatively; this industry has been oversold to before.

- Administrative relief on a well-chosen recurring task: meaningful fractions of
  an instructor's weekly admin load, not elimination of it.
- Attrition detection: value scales with how much earlier the flag arrives and
  whether an intervention exists. Without a defined intervention, value is zero.
- Collection improvement: driven mainly by consistency of follow-up rather than
  by sophistication of targeting.
- Payback expectations must fit a term or a season. Institutions do not evaluate
  on multi-year horizons, and cannot fund them.

---

# Value Framing for Two Company Types

**EdTech product companies** realise value as reduced churn and reduced cost to
serve. An AI capability that makes their customers' administration lighter is
a retention feature first and a differentiator second. Value should be expressed
per institution served, then multiplied.

**Education providers** realise value directly as time, retention and cash.
Value should be expressed per instructor and per term, in units the owner
already tracks informally.
