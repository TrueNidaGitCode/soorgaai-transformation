# Purpose

This layer supplies sports academy context for sequencing AI initiatives: what
makes something a defensible starting point in a coaching business, and which
apparently attractive initiatives should be deliberately deferred.

Prioritisation method and output structure are inherited from the Core Asset.

---

## AI Implementation Prioritization

Two constraints dominate sequencing here, and both push toward smaller first
steps than an enterprise plan would suggest.

**There is no implementation team.** The people who would run a pilot are the
four administrators already running the academy, between phone calls. An
initiative requiring sustained internal effort will stall, however sound.

**The evaluation window is a season, not a year.** If value is not visible
within one fee cycle or one term, the initiative is judged to have failed
regardless of its long-run merit — and the next season's cash is already
committed to coaches and grounds.

So the first initiative should be one where the data already exists, the
workflow already exists, and an admin already performs the task by hand — AI
removes effort from something happening this week rather than introducing
something new.

And it should sit at the right step of the progression. The academy is
understood before it is answered, answered before it is advised, advised
before anything is prepared for it, and prepared for before anything is done
on its behalf. Sequencing that skips a step produces a demo the admins
admire and do not trust.

# Prioritisation Criteria for Sports Academies

Weight these more heavily than generic effort/impact scoring:

1. **Data already captured.** Attendance, the roster and the fee ledger are
   recorded because the business cannot run without them. Initiatives built on
   them start immediately. Initiatives that need video, skill scores or
   wearables start after a behaviour change that usually does not happen.
2. **Relieves the admin team.** In a multi-coach academy the admins are the
   constraint on everything. Rank by hours taken off them per week, not by
   sophistication.
3. **Existing workflow to attach to.** The output must land where an admin
   already looks — the roster, the fee list, the parent thread. A new place to
   look is a new habit, and new habits fail at the ground.
4. **Unpleasantness relieved.** Adoption tracks disliked work removed more
   reliably than time saved. Fee follow-up, roster repair and repetitive
   parent messaging are the strongest candidates on this criterion.
5. **Reversibility.** An initiative an admin can ignore on a chaotic Saturday
   will be trusted. One that blocks a session if it misbehaves will be switched
   off by Sunday.
6. **Independence from data cleanup.** Anything gated on tidying three years of
   spreadsheets inherits an unbounded prerequisite.
7. **Weekly recurrence.** Prefer initiatives whose value recurs every week
   over those that matter only at camp enrolment or trials.
8. **Coach neutrality.** Prefer initiatives that do not ask coaches to change
   what they record or how, and never ones that appear to grade their players
   for them.

# Typical Sequencing

A defensible order for a first season, adjusted to the academy:

**Start with understanding, delivered as answers.** Join the form, the
spreadsheet, the payment app and the batch groups into one picture, and let
the admins ask it: who is expected tomorrow, who has not replied, whose plan
lapses this month, who played in the last three matches. The data is there,
nobody records anything new, and the first correct answer to a question that
used to take three windows is the proof. The expected-attendance summary from
the day-before thread — coming, not coming, not yet answered — is the
sharpest first deliverable inside this, because it is asked every single day.

**Follow with** recommendations over the same joined data: the disengagement
flag (a player who has stopped replying, coming or paying on time), the
suggested match side that respects availability, recent participation and
fair opportunity with the coach deciding, and the renewal list. These earn
trust because the answers underneath them have already been checked by the
people who know.

**Then** prepare actions: the follow-up list for the silent parents, the
batch message, the fee reminder, the match announcement — drafted for a
person to send. Rostering and enquiry handling join here, touching more of the
operation and needing the credibility earned earlier.

**Last**, and only with permissions and safeguards, act: send the reminder,
post the summary, update the record.

**Defer** anything assessing a player's technique, ability or selection. It is
the most requested and the least suitable first step: it needs new capture, it
is the coach's craft, it is the thing parents are most sensitive about, and it
is where coaches are least willing to accept a machine's view before trust
exists. Video analysis belongs here, last.

# Effort Realities in This Industry

- Integration effort is dominated by messy, split records — an
  academy-management app, spreadsheets, the accounts package and WhatsApp —
  not by modelling.
- Change management is small in headcount but decisive: convincing the four
  admins and the two most senior coaches is the whole rollout, and one vocal
  sceptic on the coaching staff can end it.
- Infrastructure expectations are nil; an academy will not run anything
  itself. Delivery must be hosted and operated for them, and reachable from a
  phone at the ground.
- Budgets are small and seasonal, aligned to fee collection and to the
  summer-camp windfall.
- Weather and exams will interrupt any pilot. Plan for a season with gaps in
  it.
