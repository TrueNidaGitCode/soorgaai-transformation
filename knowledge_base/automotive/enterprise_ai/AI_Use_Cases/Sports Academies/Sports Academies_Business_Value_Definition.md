# Purpose

This layer supplies sports academy context for quantifying the value of an AI
initiative: which measures an academy owner actually watches, what the
realistic magnitudes are, and which apparent benefits do not survive contact
with how a coaching business makes money.

Valuation method and output structure are inherited from the Core Asset.

---

## Business Value Definition

An academy is a fee-per-player business with a fixed cost of coaches and
venues and a small admin team holding it together. Value has to be expressed
in the owner's own units: players retained, batches filled, fees collected on
time, admin hours given back. "Productivity" means nothing at the boundary
rope.

### The value underneath the levers

The deeper value in this industry is not any one task automated. It is a
persistent understanding of how the academy works — its students, parents,
coaches, batches, schedules, payments, attendance, matches, venues, its
decisions and exceptions and the rules its staff carry in their heads — kept
current and usable. Today that understanding exists only in people; when a
coach or an admin leaves, it leaves with them. Value it as what the academy
could not previously do at all: answer a question across its systems in
seconds, explain why a side was picked, notice a family drifting. Then count
the levers below as the places that understanding shows up in the numbers.

### The four value levers that matter

**1. Retention.** The dominant lever. A player who leaves takes every future
month's fee with them, and replacing them means winning a family in a local,
word-of-mouth market. A family that leaves quietly usually takes a sibling and
a friend. Anything that catches disengagement early is measured here.

**2. Admin hours returned.** In a thirty-coach academy the four administrators
are the ceiling on growth: every new batch adds roster, fee and parent load to
the same four people. Hours removed from a recurring admin task are hours the
academy can spend on enrolment, on parents, or simply on finishing before nine
at night — and they are what lets the academy add its next ten coaches
without a fifth admin.

**3. Collection rate and timing.** Fees owed but not collected are the working
capital problem in this industry. Coaches and grounds are paid monthly whether
or not the families have. Faster, more consistent follow-up turns revenue
already earned into cash without a single new enrolment.

**4. Utilisation.** An empty lane, an under-filled batch or an idle coach at a
booked ground is unrecoverable revenue. Better rostering and honest batch
fill numbers recover it.

# Measures That Work in This Industry

- Players retained per term, and how many weeks before drop-out the risk was
  flagged
- Admin hours per week on rostering, fee follow-up and parent messaging
- Roster changes handled per week, and time from cancellation to fixed roster
- Fees collected within the month; days outstanding
- Batch fill rate against capacity, and lane or ground utilisation
- Enquiry-to-trial and trial-to-enrolment conversion, and time to first reply
- Coach sessions delivered versus rostered; substitutions per month
- Parent-communication coverage: families reached with a progress update per
  term

---

# Measures That Mislead in This Industry

- **Messages sent.** Volume is not value. A parent who receives four automated
  messages a week mutes the group, and the one that mattered is lost.
- **Model accuracy in isolation.** A drop-out flag nobody calls about is worth
  nothing. Value accrues at the phone call, not the prediction.
- **Dashboard views.** Owners and admins are at the ground. Low dashboard use
  says nothing about whether the insight was acted on.
- **Aggregate minutes saved.** Minutes scattered across a day do not reassemble
  into an hour. Value is credible only where a single recurring task gets
  visibly shorter.
- **Player performance improvement.** Real, but it belongs to the coach, and
  claiming it for software loses the coaches.
- **Tools replaced.** Retiring the spreadsheet is not value; the spreadsheet
  was free. Value is what the academy can now know and do across the tools
  it keeps.

# Realistic Magnitudes

Anchor estimates conservatively; academies have been sold "management apps"
that became one more place to type.

- Admin relief on a well-chosen recurring task: a meaningful fraction of the
  team's weekly load on that task, not its elimination. Rostering and parent
  messaging are the tasks where the fraction is largest.
- Retention: value scales with how early the flag arrives and whether the
  academy has a defined response — a coach's call, a trial in a different
  batch, a fee plan. Without a defined response, value is zero.
- Collection: driven mainly by consistency of follow-up rather than by
  cleverness of targeting.
- Payback must fit a term or a season. An academy evaluates in months and
  budgets from the last fee cycle.

# Value Framing by Academy Shape

**Owner-run academies** realise value as the founder's evenings and as
players not lost. Express it per week and per term.

**Multi-coach academies** realise value as admin capacity: the number of
coaches and batches the same four people can run, and the retention and cash
that a less overloaded team recovers. Express it per admin per week, and as
batches supported per admin.

**Franchise academies** realise value as consistency across centres and as
reporting that arrives without a centre manager assembling it.
