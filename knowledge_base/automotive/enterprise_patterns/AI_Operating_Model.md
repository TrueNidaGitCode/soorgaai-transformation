# AI Operating Model — Enterprise Pattern

**Type:** Enterprise Pattern  
**Reusable across:** All business domains

---

## Pattern Overview

Defines how an organization structures its people, processes, and technology to
deliver and sustain AI transformation at scale.

---

## Operating Model Components

### 1. Organizational Structure
How AI roles and responsibilities are distributed across central and domain teams.

Recommended: Hub-and-Spoke (see `AI_CoE.md`)

### 2. Delivery Model
How AI initiatives are conceived, built, deployed, and improved.

Recommended stages:
- Discover → Validate → Build → Deploy → Scale → Optimise

### 3. Governance Model
How AI decisions are made and risks are managed.

See: `AI_Governance.md`

### 4. Technology Model
The shared platforms, tools, and infrastructure that enable AI delivery.

Core components:
- Data platform
- Model development environment
- MLOps pipeline
- Model registry and monitoring

### 5. Talent Model
How AI skills are acquired, developed, and retained.

Three talent streams:
- AI practitioners (data scientists, ML engineers)
- AI-enabled engineers (domain engineers with AI skills)
- AI-aware leaders (executives and managers who can sponsor and govern AI)

---

## Operating Model Maturity

| Stage | Characteristics |
|-------|----------------|
| Ad Hoc | Individual AI experiments, no shared infrastructure |
| Establishing | CoE formed, first shared platform, initial standards |
| Operating | Hub-and-spoke active, repeatable delivery process |
| Scaling | Enterprise-wide adoption, automated governance, self-service AI |
| Optimising | Continuous improvement, AI improving AI, competitive differentiation |

---

## Transition Planning

Moving from Ad Hoc to Operating typically takes 12–24 months.
Key transitions:
- Individual experiments → Portfolio management
- Manual governance → Automated compliance checks
- Bespoke infrastructure → Shared AI platform
- Skills scarcity → Systematic capability building
