# AI ROI — Enterprise Pattern

**Type:** Enterprise Pattern  
**Reusable across:** All business domains

---

## Pattern Overview

Provides a reusable framework for measuring, attributing, and communicating the
return on AI investment across business domains and enterprise programs.

---

## ROI Measurement Dimensions

| Dimension | What it Measures |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Efficiency | Time, cost, and resource savings from AI automation |
| Quality | Defect reduction, rework avoidance, reliability improvement |
| Revenue | New revenue or accelerated revenue from AI capabilities |
| Risk | Risk reduction value (compliance, safety, operational) |
| Strategic | Competitive positioning and capability building value |

---

## Measurement Approach

### Establish Baseline
Before AI deployment, capture the current state metrics that the AI initiative will affect.
No baseline = no credible ROI claim.

### Define Value Hypothesis
For each initiative, state: "If we deploy [AI capability], we expect [measurable outcome] 
within [time period] because [mechanism]."

### Measure and Report
Track agreed metrics post-deployment. Report monthly to domain leads, quarterly to
program level, annually to executive level.

### Validate Attribution
Distinguish AI contribution from other changes occurring simultaneously.
Use control groups where feasible. Be conservative in attribution claims.

---

## Quick Reference: ROI by Domain

| Domain | Primary ROI Driver |
|--------|-------------------|
| Diagnostics | Warranty cost reduction, workshop efficiency |
| ADAS | Test cycle compression, defect escape reduction |
| Validation | Test automation ROI, regression detection speed |
| Manufacturing | Quality defect reduction, equipment uptime |
| Connectivity | Service revenue enablement, OTA reliability |

---

## Anti-Patterns

- Claiming ROI before production deployment
- Measuring only cost (missing revenue and strategic value)
- Conflating correlation with AI attribution
- Not establishing a baseline before deployment
