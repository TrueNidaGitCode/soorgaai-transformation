# Regulations — Automotive AI

Key regulatory frameworks affecting AI deployment in automotive organizations.

---

## EU AI Act

**Status:** In force (phased implementation 2024–2027)  
**Relevance:** High — affects all automotive AI systems deployed in the EU market

### High-Risk AI Systems in Automotive (Annex III)
- AI used as safety components in vehicles covered by EU type-approval regulations
- AI in ADAS and automated driving systems

### Requirements for High-Risk AI Systems
- Risk management system throughout the lifecycle
- Data governance and data quality requirements
- Technical documentation
- Transparency and logging
- Human oversight provisions
- Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity requirements
- Conformity assessment before market placement
- Post-market monitoring

### Strategic Implication
Automotive enterprises must establish EU AI Act compliance programs now.
High-risk AI systems in ADAS and safety functions require conformity assessment —
a process that takes 12–18 months to complete.

---

## ISO 26262 — Functional Safety

**Scope:** Electrical and electronic systems in road vehicles  
**Relevance:** Mandatory for AI in safety-critical vehicle functions

AI components integrated into safety functions must be part of the safety case.
The standard does not fully cover AI/ML-specific risks (see SOTIF for that).

---

## ISO 21448 — SOTIF

**Scope:** Safety of the Intended Functionality for ADAS and automated driving  
**Relevance:** High for ADAS and Automated Driving AI

SOTIF addresses safety risks caused by insufficiencies in the intended functionality —
i.e., what happens when the AI system behaves as designed but the design is insufficient.
Critical for perception systems, object classification, and decision-making AI.

---

## ISO 21434 — Cybersecurity

**Scope:** Road vehicle cybersecurity  
**Relevance:** Connected vehicle AI, V2X, OTA systems

AI systems that process vehicle data or interface with connectivity systems must
be assessed for cybersecurity risk. ISO 21434 requires cybersecurity management
throughout the vehicle lifecycle.

---

## GDPR

**Scope:** Processing of personal data of EU residents  
**Relevance:** Any AI system processing driver, passenger, or vehicle owner data

Automotive AI systems processing biometric data (driver monitoring), location data,
or behavioural data require explicit consent, data minimisation, and right-to-erasure compliance.

---

## UNECE WP.29 — Automated Vehicles

**Scope:** Type approval regulations for automated driving systems  
**Relevance:** OEMs deploying Level 3+ automated driving in regulated markets

UNECE WP.29 regulations (UN R155 Cybersecurity, UN R156 Software Updates) are mandatory
for automated vehicle deployment in the UN regulatory framework.

---

## IATF 16949 / ISO 9001

**Scope:** Automotive quality management systems  
**Relevance:** AI in manufacturing and production quality

AI tools integrated into quality-controlled manufacturing processes must be validated
and documented within the IATF 16949 quality management system.
